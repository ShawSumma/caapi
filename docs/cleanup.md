# Cleanup log

Working document for the fudge audit. Each item: what is there, what the
program actually calls for, the plan, and status. Items that touch only the
windowed client cannot be verified headlessly; they are marked "needs a
visual check".

Verification tools: `dub test`, the Lua scripts in `tests/`, and
`--dump-world` byte comparison for worldgen.

## Geometry facts used below

- Distance to a geodesic plane is 1-Lipschitz: |d(p,P) - d(q,P)| <= d(p,q).
- A chunk is geodesically convex (its faces are geodesic planes) and distance
  to a point is convex along geodesics, so the farthest point of a chunk from
  any point is one of its 8 corners.
- `NodeState.surface` is one global plane (the side-A faces of the
  right-angled honeycomb tile it), so altitude is frame independent and can
  be compared across nodes.
- Moving along `up` (the gradient of the plane distance) by h changes the
  altitude by exactly h: `up` points along the perpendicular geodesic.
- Chunk edges have valence 4 when either face is a node side (low face) and
  valence 5 when both faces are node-interior (high faces: the edge from a
  dodecahedron face center to the node center). Corners: dodecahedron vertex
  and edge midpoint 8, face center 10, node center 20. A "diagonal neighbor"
  is only well defined on the valence-4 edges.

## Items

1. Fog off (unit mix-up). DONE, verified offscreen. The fog shader's
   distance is atanh of the Klein length, i.e. absolute units, but the
   density divided by 75 (meters, presumably), so fog reached its 1e-3
   visibility at 75 absolute units against a 4.5 render radius: no fog.
   What the fog is for is hiding the end of the loaded world, so it now
   reaches that visibility exactly at `LocalWorld.renderRadius`. The
   exponent (5) is sent in the UBO instead of being repeated in the
   shader.
2. Fast-path solid chunks are Dirt instead of layered material. DONE.
   The shortcut existed to skip per-voxel work for buried chunks, but it
   stored the wrong content. Generating everything exactly cost 4x the
   worldgen CPU (A/B, three runs each: ~665 ms vs ~150 ms at radius 2.5),
   for material only visible when digging. Now:
   - a chunk is stored uniform only when proven uniform: entirely below the
     surface layer, in one elevation band, and its rain/temperature range
     (with noise bounds) inside one lattice cell (Voronoi cells are
     convex, so four corners suffice);
   - a chunk proven completely full but not uniform is stored solid with
     `materialsDeferred`; its exact materials are generated by the same
     deterministic function the moment it can be seen (an edit in it, or
     a void cell appearing in its margin);
   - dense chunks whose content turns out uniform are compressed.
   Proof: `--dump-world` resolves every deferred chunk before hashing, and
   the result is byte-identical to eager exact generation. Cost: ~215-250
   ms (three runs; the first is slowest, clock ramp-up under `powersave`).
   Zero deferred chunks were exposed during generation at radius 2.5.
   Road supports now only fill air; they used to overwrite ground in
   partially filled chunks but not in solid ones, depending on chunk
   boundaries.
3. Node crossings drop eye smoothing, odometer and bounce measurements;
   the bounce metric also mixes camera and body frames. DONE. All three
   wanted a frame-free quantity and used node-local coordinates instead:
   - vertical motion is altitude, the distance to the one global surface
     plane, so the grounded rise is an altitude difference and works across
     nodes;
   - the camera sits `eyeOffset - eyeLag` along up from the body, and
     moving along up changes altitude by exactly that, so camera altitude is
     body altitude plus that offset, and the bounce metric is its second
     difference; no frames are mixed;
   - the controller returns the path length it moved, summed over its
     substeps before each node hop.
   The rise also used `wasOnGround || onGround`, which fed the last frame
   of a fall into the eye lag on landing; it is now `&&` (ground to ground:
   steps up and down).
   The odometer took `acosh(-mip)` in float, which for a frame's ~0.003
   absolute units has ~1% error, and reports ~7 mm per frame standing still
   (acosh of 1 + one ulp). It is now `asinh` of the spatial part, which is
   well conditioned at small distances. Traveled distances in the tests
   dropped by up to 4%.
4. AO diagonal reads clamped margin cells. DONE (falls out of 5).
   Meshing reads neighbors directly, so ambient occlusion no longer
   depends on margin edge cells (which hold whichever neighbor populated
   last, so they were also nondeterministic; nothing reads them now).
   `common/chunk_neighbors.d` resolves a padded cell outside the
   interior by hopping through slot-based chunk neighbors (no hash
   lookups) in every axis order; where orders disagree (valence-5 edges)
   it takes a solid cell. A test checks the geometry against the
   honeycomb: on all 180 chunk edges involving a node side both hop
   orders reach the same cell, and on all 60 node-interior edges they
   reach different chunks. A chunk's mesh depends on every chunk sharing
   a face, edge or corner, so population and boundary edits bump those
   chunks' generations (about 2 us per population).
5. Faces buffer: 2.7 GiB host-visible, all buffers host-visible. DONE,
   verified offscreen. The buffer existed because faces were extracted on
   the GPU, which cannot allocate, so every one of 65536 slots reserved
   the worst case (5616 faces). Measured, the full render radius has
   177594 meshable chunks, 370452 faces in total, at most 461 in a chunk.
   Extraction is now CPU code (`client/meshing.d`, a port of the compute
   shader, 5 us per chunk on average and 35 us worst, `--bench-mesh`),
   run under a 1 ms per frame meshing budget, nearest chunks first. Faces
   go into one device-local buffer through a range allocator; it grows by
   doubling instead of capping, and so do the staging, indirect and
   instance buffers. The CPU writes the indirect commands and per-chunk
   transforms (and the winding parity, a per-chunk constant the vertex
   shader used to recompute per vertex with a 4x4 determinant). Meshes
   are kept for every chunk in the nearby set, so turning around no
   longer re-extracts everything. Removed: the extraction and transform
   compute shaders, the voxel upload, the per-slot indirect buffer and
   its pack copies, and `MAX_RENDER_SLOTS`. The old code also issued
   multi-draw indirect with non-zero first instance without enabling
   either feature; device selection now requires both and enables them.
   GPU-only buffers are device-local; host-written ones stay mapped.
6. All materials render the same gray. DONE, verified offscreen. A
   per-material sRGB color fills each texture layer (the layers and
   sampler stay, so real textures can replace them). The colors are my
   choice.
7. Light direction and time uploaded but unused. DONE. Removed from the
   UBO, the renderer and `render`'s signature.
8. Crosshair inside the fog shader; fog color duplicates the clear color.
   DONE, verified offscreen. The crosshair is its own pipeline in the
   second subpass (three quads, so the center is not blended twice) with
   ordinary alpha blending; the fog pipeline's blend is named
   `transmittance`, since its alpha is visibility. Sky and fog share one
   color, sent in the UBO.
9. Eye lag limit hard-coded in absolute units. DONE. The lag exists to
   hide the step-up and step-down snaps, so the eye never lags more than
   one step: the limit is `stepHeight`.
10. Spawn height hard-coded, along node +y, ignores terrain. DONE. The
    spawn was 1.4 absolute along the root's +y in fly mode (5.07 m above
    the base plane, about 8 m above that column's ground), with nothing
    guaranteeing it clear of terrain. Now:
    - the worldgen's void proof is one function, `chunkProvenClear`
      (the terrain ceiling from the corner elevations plus the terrace
      and noise bounds, roads up to their thickness, road supports never
      clear), used by generation itself;
    - `clearSpawnPosition` climbs the perpendicular from the root to the
      surface plane, one body reach at a time, until every chunk the body
      could touch is proven clear (53.7 m on the default terrain; the
      bound is loose);
    - the game then generates the chunks around the body synchronously
      and settles it down with the controller's own `settle`, one body
      reach per step, until it lands. The player starts standing in walk
      mode (altitude 0.910 m, half height plus skin).
    Choosing a worldgen in a script now creates a fresh world, since the
    spawn already populated chunks with the other generator.
    `walk.spawn` no longer waits out a fall. walk_h180's free fraction
    went from 0.75 to 0.7: from the new spawn a hillside blocks heading
    180 after ~23 m (verified: solid blocks six deep at eye height and
    above at 30 degrees). flat_step checked the apex against the step
    height; voxel tops above the base plane are geodesic planes tilted
    against gravity (the step was 0.927 m in one frame, then 0.19 m more
    while crossing the top), so it now bounds the rise per frame.
11. Silent fallbacks in math and up. DONE.
    - `normalizedPoint` returned the origin for a non-timelike vector, and
      `normalizedDirection` returned +x for a non-spacelike one; the
      orthonormalization skipped zero columns. None of these inputs can
      arise from valid geometry, so substituting a plausible value only
      hid bugs. They now divide unconditionally.
    - `getRelativeUp` returned +y when the node had no state. The node
      state is a pure function of the node, so the game ensures it for the
      node the character is in (after every step, and at creation), and
      walk/fly only ask for up where the region is populated, which
      implies the state exists.
    - `sanitizeMotionInput` filtered NaN and infinity out of input built
      from key axes and a unit quaternion, which cannot produce them. Its
      real job, keeping diagonal input at unit length, is now
      `limitToUnitLength`.
    - `characterAltitude` returned NaN without node state; same guarantee
      as up.
12. Voxel upload silently truncates or uploads nothing. DONE. Both
    branches were dead (dense chunks always have exactly (dimension +
    2)^3 cells), and the upload itself is gone with GPU extraction (5).
    `matrixInverse` returned its input for a singular matrix; it only
    inverts the projection, which is never singular, so it divides
    unconditionally.
13. Vulkan results unchecked; first GPU and first surface format. DONE.
    The offscreen path is verified; the swapchain path (device ranking
    with a surface, sRGB format selection, present and resize handling)
    still needs a run of the windowed client.
    - Every creation, allocation, bind, map and submit is checked, and a
      memory type that does not exist fails setup instead of being passed
      to `vkAllocateMemory`; per-frame failures (fence wait, acquire,
      submit, present) stop the loop and the client exits with status 1.
      A minimized window (zero extent) is not a failure.
    - The device is the best-ranked one (discrete, integrated, virtual,
      other) that has a graphics queue that can present.
    - The swapchain must be `B8G8R8A8_SRGB` or `R8G8B8A8_SRGB` in sRGB
      nonlinear; setup fails otherwise. With `formats[0]` the output
      depended on the driver's ordering: the texture is sRGB, so sampled
      values are linear, and a UNORM swapchain shows them too dark.
      Constant colors are now given in sRGB and converted (sky, outline).
14. Worldgen workers share one condition variable for two predicates.
    DONE. `workAvailable` (workers wait, the driver broadcasts) and
    `resultsReady` (the driver waits, a worker signals per result).
15. Drive bench polls with sleeps. DONE. It blocks on `resultsReady` while
    work is in flight and finishes when nothing is in flight and the scan
    cursor reached the end of the nearby set, which is the actual
    definition of "settled". The three-idle-frames heuristic, the frame
    cap and `nanosleep` are gone.
16. Renderer chunk table is a growing flat array. DONE. The graph's slab
    is now a generic `Slab(T)` (`common/slab.d`: fixed 4096-entry blocks,
    stable pointers, new blocks initialized to `T.init`), used by the
    graph and by the renderer's per-chunk mesh table, which was a
    realloc'd array of nodeCount * 20 entries.
17. Movement modes are two booleans for three modes. DONE. `MovementMode
    {walk, fly, noclip}` in `CharacterInput` and `Game`; V toggles
    walk/fly, N toggles noclip/fly. The booleans allowed `noClip` with a
    hidden `flyMode` that V flipped invisibly.
18. Terrain material tables are coordinate lists of a regular lattice.
    DONE. The 113 points form a checkerboard lattice (15 rows, 8 or 7
    points). Tables are now material lists in lattice order; coordinates are
    computed; the nearest point is found in a +/-2 window visited in index
    order with the original `<=` rule, so ties resolve identically. A
    temporary test checked all 904 entries (material, both coordinates,
    order) against the old tables; a permanent test checks the search
    against brute force on ~95k queries plus every tie point. Worldgen
    byte-identical; dense chunks 0.456 -> 0.340 ms.
19. Frame time clamped to 0.1 s. DONE. The clamp made game time run slow
    on any slow frame, and it was only in the client, so scripts never
    saw it. What a long frame actually threatens is the controller's
    assumptions: the populated-region check covers one body plus one step
    of motion, the ground follow settles at most `stepHeight` after a
    move, and up and gravity are taken at the start of a move. The
    controller now substeps so that each substep moves at most one
    character radius, from a speed bound (current speed, plus what ground
    or air steering plus gravity can add over the frame, plus a jump),
    and the populated-region reach is half height + 2 radii + step height
    + skin, which covers a substep. `tests/flat_long_frames.lua` falls 6 m
    in one 2 s frame, compares walking 2 s in 0.016 s frames vs 0.5 s
    frames (7.872 vs 7.869 m; one step per frame gives 8.00, since
    acceleration is lost), and steps up in 0.25 s frames.
20. ELEVATION_MARGIN has no derivation. DONE. Replaced by a proven bound:
    voxel elevation is within the chunk's grid-center radius of the center
    elevation (plane distance is 1-Lipschitz; the farthest chunk point is a
    corner); terracing moves elevation by less than strength * scale per
    term; noise is bounded because Box-Muller over 24-bit uniforms gives
    |n| <= sqrt(2 ln(2^24 + 2)). The old 0.7 never changed geometry in
    the sample, but nothing guaranteed it.
21. Bounding radii and the dual-to-chunk factor are literals. DONE. The
    factor is computed from the dodecahedral frame (worldgen
    byte-identical). The two radii stay literals because CTFE has no acosh,
    but a test now checks them against the geometry, plus the new
    grid-center radius.
22. Unnamed shader constants; backface culling off. DONE, verified
    offscreen. Packing widths, shifts, occlusion levels, axis offsets, the
    margin and the outline scale are named. Culling: the face tables give
    a right-handed normal pointing into the void, and the vertex shader
    swaps to the reversed table exactly when det(view_projection *
    transform) < 0, so in NDC e1 x e2 always points out of the solid. The
    projection is reverse-Z (near 1) and flips y (det < 0), so a visible
    face has a positive NDC z normal, which is negative area in Vulkan's
    formula: clockwise. The voxel pipeline culls back faces with
    `frontFace = CLOCKWISE`, and the captures show every face.

## Other findings

- `--bench-gen` printed NaN for every timing: `ChunkPass` doubles default
  to NaN in D. Fixed.
- `--bench-gen` `dense_avg` divided total voxel time (solid chunks
  included) by the dense count. Now it measures dense chunks only, and the
  output adds deferred and resolved-on-exposure counts.
- `--dump-world` hashed the storage format (solid vs dense, margins), so
  equal content could hash differently. It now hashes interior materials
  and occupancy separately and ends with a digest of each, which is what
  proves shape unchanged when materials legitimately change.
- Benchmarks here run under the `powersave` governor; single samples are
  not trustworthy. Compare with repeated, alternated runs.
- Odometer precision (see 3): `acosh` near 1 in float.
- `graph.normalizeTransform` stops at a missing neighbor and leaves the
  position outside its node. The world creates nodes around the camera,
  so this needs a move past the created set, but nothing guarantees it
  (noclip at high speed). TODO: decide whether the controller may create
  nodes or must stop at the edge of the graph like it stops at unloaded
  chunks.
- `regionPopulated` silently stopped visiting nodes at 64 and could then
  answer "populated" for a region it never checked. Overflow now answers
  "not populated" (the body waits), and `capsuleOverlapsVoxel` overflow
  answers "overlaps" (placement refused). `shapeCast` (cap 128) searches
  balls inside the one `regionPopulated` checked from the same start
  node, so it cannot overflow once that succeeded; the controller always
  checks it first with a reach covering every sweep of the substep. The
  test suite's results are unchanged, so 64 is never reached there.
- `chunkShapeCast` collected faces into a 256-entry array and silently
  dropped the rest; the sweep range is several cells of padding on each
  side, so rough terrain can exceed it, and a dropped face is one the
  body passes through. Faces are now tested as they are found (sections
  are computed once, on the stack, sized by the section count). One test
  moved by 0.01 m (walk_pan), from the face-major order breaking a tie
  differently or from a face the cap used to drop.
- `VERTICAL_FOV_RADIANS` (45 degrees) was used as a half angle: the
  vertical field of view is 90 degrees. Renamed
  `VERTICAL_HALF_FOV_RADIANS`.
- Visual verification: `./mined --screenshot <png> --out <report>
  <script>` renders offscreen (no window, no input grab) after the
  script, once worldgen has covered the nearby set and meshing has no
  backlog, and writes a PNG. Scenes are in `tests/visual/`. Checked:
  culling (all faces present), material colors, AO at block bases and
  terrain steps, fog fading the terrain into the sky at the render
  radius, the outline and the crosshair. In hyperbolic space a plane
  seen from height h covers only a cone of half angle atan(1/sinh h)
  below you, so from 32 m most of a downward view is sky; that is
  geometry, not a culling bug.
- Worldgen pacing: the driver requests at most 32 chunks a frame. The
  full render radius is 201020 chunks, which the workers generate in
  2.8 s (`--bench-drive 4.5`), but at 32 a frame it takes about 6300
  frames, over 100 s at 60 fps. The request budget should follow worker
  capacity, not a constant. TODO.
- The first meshing budget counted from the start of the chunk scan, so
  once the near chunks were meshed the scan itself used the budget and
  distant chunks never meshed. The offscreen capture never finishing is
  what showed it; the budget now counts only time spent meshing.
- Collision and ray casting use NaN as "no hit" (`contactTime`,
  `solvePointPlaneIntersection`, and their callers in seven modules). It
  works because NaN compares false, but it is an implicit convention; the
  tanh parameterization already has a natural "no hit" (>= 1). TODO.
